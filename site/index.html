Method & limits

How a pick is produced

  1. Filter the universe for liquidity and history — ≥252 sessions, ≥₹10 cr median 20-day traded value, price ≥₹50.
  2. Apply the model's eligibility rules using only data known at that close.
  3. Rank survivors by a weighted score of momentum, relative strength, volume, structure, breakout quality and sector.
  4. Take the top 20; derive an ATR-based stop and an R-multiple target.

In a bear regime the momentum rules tighten automatically: ≥90% of the 52-week high and RS ≥ +5%, instead of the usual 85% / 0%.

Why the backtest ignores today's list

Momentum rules select stocks because they already rose. Replaying those same stocks' past gains would measure the selection, not the rule — the result is guaranteed to look good and means nothing.

So the engine instead replays the rules on every past scan date using only data available that day, enters at the next open, and measures what happened afterwards.

Limits you should not forget

  • Survivorship bias. The universe is today's index list. Companies that were delisted or dropped out are missing, so every backtest number here is optimistic. Dated snapshots are being archived from now on to fix this going forward.
  • No fundamentals. No delivery %, results dates, pledges or news. Those scoring weights were re-distributed across the price-based components.
  • Execution. Fills assumed at the next open. Real fills face slippage, circuit limits and gaps. When stop and target are hit the same day, the stop is assumed.
  • Sector strength is derived from universe members, not official sector indices.
